Quick workouts by event

Twelve weeks from the first practice to championships

Four phases take you from easy running to your fastest race: base, build, sharpen, and peak. A lighter week at the end of each of the first two phases gives your body time to absorb the work. Once the season starts, everyone runs without stopping. When you get tired, slow down, but keep going. Weekly miles climb from 15 to a peak of 23, with lighter weeks in weeks 4 and 8 and a taper in week 12. By week 7, most runs are 3.5 miles or more, and the long run is close to twice the race distance.

WeeksFocusWhat it looks like
1–4 BaseEasy running and formMostly easy runs. One long run a week, about a quarter of the week’s running time. Strides after easy runs, one short hill session, and a light threshold run by week 3. Week 4 is lighter.
5–8 BuildThreshold and intervalsOne hard workout a week, switching between threshold (comfortably hard) and intervals (3–5 min reps), plus a shorter second day of hills or strides. Meets count as hard days. Week 8 is lighter.
9–11 SharpenRace paceReps at goal race pace with a little less volume. Keep the hills and strides. Surge over the last 300 m of reps.
12 PeakRest and sharpenCut running time by about a third. Keep a few short, fast reps early in the week and a short shake-out the day before championships.

How to read the table
  • Mon: short, fast reps at about 80% effort. Keep them relaxed.
  • Wed: the main workout. On meet weeks, the meet is your workout. Keep Monday’s reps short and make Thursday a shake-out jog.
  • Sat: the long run, about a quarter of the week. It reaches 5.5 miles, close to twice the race distance. Slow is fine. Stopping is not.
  • By week 7, most runs are 3.5 miles or more, so the 3-mile race is shorter than a normal run.
  • Friday is a rest day until week 10, when you add a 1.5-mile easy run.
  • No one runs at 100% effort except on the final rep of a workout.
  • Not ready for the full reps? Do about two-thirds of them, with extra rest.
What a meet week looks like (week 9, race on Wednesday)

Mon 4: 4 × 200 m at 80%, short and relaxed. Tue 4 easy. Wed race (3 miles plus warm-up and cool-down, about 5). Thu 2.5 shake-out. Fri rest. Sat 5.5 long. Sun rest. About 21 miles, the same as a week without a meet.

What a meet week looks like (week 9, race on Wednesday)

Mon 4.5: 4 × 200 m at 80%, short and relaxed. Tue 4.5 easy with 4 strides. Wed race (3 miles plus warm-up and cool-down, about 5.5). Thu 3 shake-out. Fri 2 easy. Sat 7.5 long. Sun rest. About 27 miles. If you’re tired on Saturday, shorten the long run.

Eight weeks to get ready for the first practice

Easy running, built up week by week. The goal is simple: by the first day of the season, you run the whole workout without stopping. Weeks 4 and 8 are lighter on purpose. Starting out? Choose Starter above for the run-walk version. Already running strong? Choose Challenge for more miles.

Weekly miles are approximate. Run easy enough that you can talk in full sentences. Slow is fine. Stopping is not: if you get tired, shuffle, but keep moving. Take a mile test in week 2 and again in week 8, then put your times in the pace calculator to get your paces.

Your week
  • Mon, Tue, Thu: easy runs. In weeks 1–4, strides and hill sprints go after Tuesday’s run.
  • Wed: a rest day in weeks 1–4. In weeks 5–7, add a fifth run, which is where the hills go.
  • Tue and Thu: strength after your run, Strength A on Tuesday and Strength B on Thursday.
  • Sat: the long run. From week 5, finish the last half mile at a steady effort.
  • Fri and Sun: rest, or an easy walk.

Strength days (twice a week, 20–25 minutes)
  • Strength A: squats 3 × 8, split squats 3 × 8 each leg, hip thrusts or glute bridges 3 × 10, calf raises 3 × 12, planks 3 × 30–45 sec.
  • Strength B: step-ups 3 × 8 each leg, single-leg deadlifts 3 × 8 each leg, wall sits 2 × 30 sec, toe raises 3 × 15, dead bugs 3 × 10, side planks 2 × 30 sec each side.
  • Start with your body weight. Add weight only when your form stays clean and a coach is watching.

Strength day and event add-ons
  • Strength day: pick from the Strength workouts section further down. Strength A and Strength B in the cross country plan are good starting points.
  • Sprinters and hurdlers: add 4 × 10 m falling starts and hurdle walkover drills on Day 3.
  • Jumpers: add 6 × 20 m approach runs and takeoff drills on Day 3.
  • Throwers: add medicine ball throws, 3 × 6 overhead and 3 × 6 rotational, on Days 2 and 3.
  • Distance runners: build Day 1 up to a 35- to 40-minute easy run by week 6.
  • Land softly on both feet. Stop jumping when your form gets sloppy, and tell Coach if anything hurts.
